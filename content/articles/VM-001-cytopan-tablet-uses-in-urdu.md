Understanding how medications work in the body involves pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics. Pharmacokinetics refers to the movement of drug into, through, and out of the body—the time course of its absorption, bioavailability, distribution, metabolism, and excretion. Pharmacodynamics, on the other hand, is the study of the biochemical and physiologic effects of drugs and their mechanisms of action.

When you take a tablet like Cytopan, it typically goes through your digestive system. It is absorbed into the bloodstream, usually through the walls of the small intestine. From there, it travels to the liver, where it may be metabolized before it reaches the rest of the body. This is known as first-pass metabolism. The active ingredients then travel through the bloodstream to the target cells or organs where they exert their therapeutic effect.
